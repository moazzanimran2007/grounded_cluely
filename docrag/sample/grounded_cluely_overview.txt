Grounded Cluely: Project Overview

Grounded Cluely is a desktop meeting assistant. It listens to a live meeting,
answers questions the user types to it, and stays out of any shared screen so
it does not appear when the user shares their display.

The assistant has three capabilities. The first is a meeting listener that
captures audio from the computer and transcribes it live into a running
transcript. The second is document context, where the user uploads files and
the assistant retrieves relevant passages to ground its answers. The third is
web search, used only when the transcript and the documents cannot answer a
question.

Task 1 is the meeting listener. It captures microphone audio using the
sounddevice library and streams it to Deepgram for live transcription. The
audio is captured as mono at a sample rate of 16000 hertz, encoded as 16 bit
PCM. The transcript is printed to the terminal in near real time, with interim
results refining in place and final results committed to their own line.

Task 2 is document context through retrieval, also called RAG. A document is
extracted to text, split into overlapping chunks of roughly 250 words, and
embedded into vectors. Each chunk is around 250 words with a 40 word overlap so
that an idea is not cut across a boundary. The vectors are stored in a small
local JSON file rather than a hosted vector database, which keeps the early
version cheap and easy to inspect. Embeddings come from the Google
gemini-embedding-001 model, and answers come from the gemini-2.5-flash model,
both on a free tier.

The retrieval step embeds the question and compares it against the stored
chunks using cosine similarity, returning the four closest chunks. The answer
step feeds those chunks and the question to the model and instructs it to answer
only from the provided context, or to say it could not find the answer when the
context does not contain it.

The whole project has a budget of about 60 hours over the summer, at roughly two
hours a day. The guiding constraints are to keep costs low by using free tiers,
to write code the author can understand and explain, and to avoid em dashes in
all prose and comments.
